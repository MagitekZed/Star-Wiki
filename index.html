How it works

Every Wikipedia article is a glowing star; its most related links orbit around it as smaller stars, joined by rays. Fall down the rabbit hole one link at a time.

Getting around the galaxy

  • Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom. Leave it alone and the view drifts gently on its own.
  • Hover a ray or star to see its title; click it for a quick preview, then click the preview to travel there.
  • The pink ray always leads back to the page you came from.
  • ← / → arrow keys step back and forward through your trail; the breadcrumbs up top jump to any stop.

Reading the map

  • Faint violet web — “cross-links”: connections between the orbiting links, showing how a topic’s neighbours relate to each other.
  • The glowing trail marks the path you’ve travelled. Visited stars look dimmer.

Toolbar

  • Search — jump to any article.
  • Surprise me — a random article to start from.
  • Find path — discover a chain of links between two articles, then fly the route.
  • Galaxy map — zoom out to see your whole journey as a constellation; click any node to fly there.
  • View options — toggle Backlinks (pages that link to this one), the Trail, the Cross-links, and scale stars by article length.
  • Recenter view and Start over (back to this welcome screen).
  • More — copy a shareable link to your journey, download a snapshot, or save journeys to revisit later.
